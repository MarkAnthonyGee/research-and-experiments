# Community problem discovery

The decision this method improves is whether a specific problem deserves more investigation. Community activity can reveal workflow failures and crowded solution categories. Its value depends on who is speaking and what the sample actually covers.

## Frame the question

Name the task, likely user and decision. For example: “Do independent service businesses have enough trouble reconciling appointment changes to justify interviews?”

Choose a bounded window before collection. For supplied evidence, report the actual window and sampling method. Select a stopping rule such as one focused review session or a fixed number of relevant accounts. These are effort limits, not thresholds that prove demand.

## Distinguish the speakers

| Source role | Useful evidence | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier or builder | Launches, implementations, founder bottlenecks, competing offers | Promotion does not establish buyer pain |
| Buyer or practitioner | Failed tasks, workarounds, switching, consequences, current spending | Accounts may be selective or unrepresentative |
| Product reviewer or support participant | Failure conditions, gaps, escalation and switching triggers | Product-specific selection bias and uncertain authenticity |

Classify the account in relation to the task. A developer describing their own submission failure may be a practitioner; the same developer selling a submission tool is a supplier. Community membership alone does not settle the distinction.

After a problem hypothesis survives, choose roughly three buyer or review sources that cover the actual role and workflow. More builder communities cannot fill an absent buyer panel.

## Record access and coverage

Use a route permitted for the intended collection and analysis. Check source terms, available fields, limits and retention requirements. See [platform access notes](../../../PROVENANCE.md#platform-access).

Record the requested window, observed window, ordering or selection, raw count, oldest item, cutoff status, errors, ceilings and missing fields. A selected set of reviews is a sample; do not describe it as a complete week.

Keep only the fields needed for the question: source reference, date, necessary text and legitimately available metadata. Avoid identities when they add no research value. Keep evidence separate from the public output, and apply required deletion handling to derived copies as well as source captures.

## Triage locally, then read selectively

For a large input, use local parsing to calculate dates and counts. Flag duplicate candidates before counting distinct accounts. State whether the rule used exact titles, normalized text or manual inspection; a matching title alone does not prove that two people are the same source. Preserve both raw and retained totals.

Use four overlapping labels to prioritize reading:

| Label | Candidate signal |
|---|---|
| Pain | Blocked task, repeated error or consequential delay |
| Workaround | Manual handoff, duplicate entry, spreadsheet or tool switching |
| Economic consequence | Reported spending, hiring, loss, churn or time cost |
| Supply | An offer, launch, promotional recommendation or proposed solution |

Keyword hits are provisional. “Lost” can describe a customer loss or a missing button. Money in a launch price is a supply signal, not proof that anyone paid. Review examples from both selected and excluded items to look for these errors. Report the extent of that review without claiming an accuracy rate you did not measure.

For model review, supply compact candidate groups and source references first. Read full accounts and available replies only when they could change the judgment. Include contradictions and weaker matches, not only the most dramatic complaints. Stop when new accounts repeat the same mechanism without changing confidence or the next step.

## Decide what the evidence earns

A recurring problem needs multiple independent accounts and a concrete failure, workaround or consequence. Separate repeated firsthand experience from copied launches, affiliate campaigns and claims about what other people supposedly need. Recurrence across sources can help; one narrowly defined practitioner segment may also be enough to justify further checking.

Write each surviving hypothesis as a user performing a task that fails under specific conditions. Describe the current workaround and observed cost. Record whether costs were self-reported, measured or unknown; do not convert minutes into revenue without evidence.

Keep a single severe incident as a lead until recurrence is checked. Keep supplier activity as a saturation observation. Neither becomes validated buyer demand through repetition alone.

Missing votes limit engagement comparisons. Missing comments limit interpretation of unresolved cases. Partial coverage prevents full-window totals and rate comparisons. Absent buyers prevents a buyer-demand conclusion. These gaps can still leave a useful, narrower hypothesis. Missing authorization prevents the affected collection; use permitted existing material or another source.

## End with a bounded next check

Save the strongest few hypotheses, exact evidence pointers, contradictions and the condition that would stop each branch. Name the practitioner role and a small next sample or interview question. Existing spend and workarounds can justify investigation; willingness to pay for a new solution remains a separate test.
