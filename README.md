# Research & Experiments

My background is in operations, project management and process improvement. I'm teaching myself how to use AI to explore questions and make things I can use. This is a growing collection of that work, with resources others may find useful too.

I use AI for research, drafting and technical implementation. My part is choosing the questions, directing the work and making sense of what comes back. Each project needs to explain what was done and what the evidence actually supports.

This is a personal collection. I'm not seeking outside contributions or offering ongoing support.

## First resource: Community Problem Radar

I went into startup and AI communities looking for ideas. The more useful finding was how much those communities revealed about problems builders encounter, and how little that alone established about buyer demand.

That observation became the starting point for this method. An AI assistant performed the collection and analysis, then drafted the guide, template and assistant instructions included here under my direction. The example is fictional, and the method hasn't been independently validated.

Turn community discussions into a small, evidence-backed list of problems worth investigating. Separate product launches from buyer experiences, record gaps in coverage, and choose a bounded next check.

**Version 0.1.** The collection begins with Community Problem Radar. The material is available under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE.md), which allows reuse and adaptation with attribution, including commercial use.

## Try it

1. Read the [guide](skills/community-problem-radar/references/guide.md).
2. Copy the [research template](skills/community-problem-radar/assets/research-template.md) into your own research folder.
3. Compare your analysis with the [worked synthetic example](skills/community-problem-radar/references/worked-example.md).

Start with material you may legitimately analyze. A text editor is sufficient. An assistant can help interpret selected evidence; no API key, database or collection tool is included or required for the example.

For an assistant that supports skills, copy the complete `skills/community-problem-radar` folder into its supported skill directory. Keep its references and assets together. Alternatively, supply [SKILL.md](skills/community-problem-radar/SKILL.md) and the referenced files directly.

Example request:

> Use Community Problem Radar on the supplied evidence. Decide whether one problem deserves buyer interviews. Separate supplier and practitioner accounts, report coverage limits, and complete the research template. Identify what would disprove the strongest hypothesis.

A useful result identifies who struggles with which task, what they do today, how strong the evidence is, and what to investigate next. A conclusion that no candidate earned further work is also useful.

## Scope and status

The method is for researchers and builders investigating recurring workflow failures. It cannot establish market size, representative prevalence or willingness to pay from a community sample. The worked example is fictional and demonstrates judgment, not market findings.

This package contains a method, template and assistant instructions. See [provenance and reuse status](PROVENANCE.md) before redistribution and the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) for version notes.
